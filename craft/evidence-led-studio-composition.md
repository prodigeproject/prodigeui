# Evidence-Led Expressive Studio Composition

Use this route when the selected quality profile is `expressiveStudio` and the brief asks a
creative studio, portfolio, agency, editorial brand, or cultural project to feel capable in
the real world. It refines the normal Design Read; it is not a fixed page template.

## The claim-to-proof route

Before composition, write four private decisions:

1. **Claim** — the proposition that should be remembered after the first viewport.
2. **Proof** — one object, work sample, material, place, interface state, campaign crop, or
   original authored visual that makes that claim credible.
3. **Source route** — user/project asset, license-cleared local media, original generated
   local media, or code-native artwork. Record provenance whenever an asset is used.
4. **Invitation** — what a prospective collaborator should bring or decide at the close.

The hero must make the claim and proof read as one statement. A monogram, gradient, or
abstract geometry may support a proposition, but it cannot be the only proof unless it
visibly models a specific material relationship or deliverable.

## Build evidence, not a gallery shell

For a multi-project studio page, select work subjects by the evidence they provide, not by a
reusable card topology. When the brief allows it, use three different delivery modes such as:

- identity in public: a campaign, poster, identity application, or publishing object;
- environment or editorial: an object, room, signage, print, spatial crop, or cultural event;
- digital behavior: a credible interface state, interaction, service moment, or broadcast surface.

Each subject needs a visible title, compact metadata, a distinct compositional role, and one
concrete cue that could not be pasted onto an unrelated studio. Do not manufacture fake case
studies, invented metrics, or equal media boxes. If a proof cannot be supported honestly,
present it as an original concept study and name its medium truthfully.

## Rhythm and visual mass

Use the sequence of information jobs rather than a fixed landing-page recipe. A strong
expressive page usually moves between a proposition with proof, a quieter belief or reading
moment, evidence at increasing density, a capability or method clarification, and a closing
invitation. The number and order are generative.

At each transition, ask whether the section earned its area through proof, reading, action,
or active framing. Make the artifact, headline, and quiet field counterweight one another.
Do not repair imbalance with badges, glow, generic gradients, or extra cards.

## Close the same argument

The closing action must resolve the original claim in practical language: say what the
visitor can bring, start, test, or make with the studio. Reuse an earlier material or signal
role, not a new color or unrelated visual system.

## Motion restraint

Motion is optional. Add one reduced-motion-safe interaction only when it reveals a material
relationship, crop, interface state, or project consequence. If motion merely announces that
the page is modern, keep the page static.

## Review checklist

- Does the first viewport pair a claim with proof rather than an abstract logo treatment?
- Is every media asset local, truthful about its origin, and integrated through crop, caption,
  palette, and neighboring type?
- Do work subjects show distinct media or delivery modes with visible identity and metadata?
- Does the full-page silhouette alternate pressure and release without empty card volume?
- Does the closing action make the same argument as the first viewport?
