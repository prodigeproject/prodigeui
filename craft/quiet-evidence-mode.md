# Quiet Evidence Mode

Use this mode when an expressive, cultural, editorial, portfolio, or studio brief should
retain a quiet, iconic, poster-like first viewport while still proving that the work can exist
in the real world. It is a composition mode, not a template and not a permission to copy a
benchmark artifact.

## The governing sequence

`claim -> one proof object -> work chapter detail -> specific invitation`

The hero should be remembered as one proposition and one object. The object may contain a
material relationship, a route, a surface, or a deliverable cue, but its supporting details must
remain subordinate. Move the full evidence taxonomy below the fold.

## Hero budget

Before code, record this private budget:

- one proposition-scale headline, normally five to eight words;
- one proof object, local and truthful, with one leading material mass;
- at most two compact supporting cues such as a caption, label, or secondary mark;
- one primary action and one short explanatory paragraph;
- one broad paper, neutral, or quiet field that visibly releases the composition;
- one main signal family plus at most one counter-accent in the first viewport.

The budget is a review limit, not a literal CSS recipe. If the hero needs a second saturated
panel, a row of equal mini-artifacts, several captions, or a dense legend to explain itself,
the proof belongs in the work chapter instead.

## Evidence without visual noise

Treat the proof as a material part of the composition: integrate it through crop, scale,
caption, palette, and neighboring type. Do not place a concept graphic beside a separate
proof dashboard. If the brief has multiple delivery modes, let the proof carry a small hint of
them and let the work chapter expand them in a `2 + 1` hierarchy: one dominant subject, one
supporting subject, and one later or quieter subject.

Keep project titles, category, year or equivalent identity, and primary metadata visible. The
quietness comes from hierarchy and omission, not from hiding evidence behind hover or from
reducing the page to an abstract monogram.

## Color and copy discipline

Prefer a light or neutral structural field, a deep text/anchor surface, and one intentional
signal relationship. Saturated color should carry the object or a meaningful chapter role;
never distribute several unrelated accent fields across the hero merely to make the proof feel
more complete. Keep the closing action in the same signal family.

## Review questions

- Can a reviewer describe the first viewport in one sentence without listing its components?
- Does the proof make the claim more credible while remaining one visual object?
- Is the empty or neutral field doing visible compositional work?
- Are detailed delivery modes deferred to the work chapter rather than competing in the hero?
- Does the candidate preserve the chosen reference's art direction and visual mass before it
  attempts to improve evidence coverage?
