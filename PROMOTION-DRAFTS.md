The thing I care about is simple: a fashion shop, a travel plan, a healthcare
journey, and a work tool should not all come out looking like the same website
with different colours.

I froze the version that felt calmest and clearest after side-by-side reviews,
then put it through 10 very different product situations.

No big promises—just a public benchmark, real visual judgement, and a reference

## LinkedIn

Most AI-made websites are not broken. They are just interchangeable.

I’ve been working on ProdigeUI to help AI-generated interfaces start from the
real product decision instead: what someone needs to understand, compare,
choose, or recover from.

We recently froze the version that felt the most calm and coherent in review,
then used it as a benchmark across 10 very different examples—from fashion and
property to travel, healthcare, finance, learning, and a release-management
tool.

The goal is not to make every product look “on brand” with ProdigeUI. The goal
is to make each product feel like itself, while keeping the interface clear and
considered.

It is still a design benchmark, not a claim about conversion or universal user
preference. But it gives us a concrete public reference for what we want to
protect while improving the system.

You can browse the benchmark here:
https://github.com/prodigeproject/prodigeui/tree/07a5ae6/benchmark/market-calibrated-fresh-20260822
