# Scorecard — quiet-capability fresh benchmark

## Result

The new rule is a **conditional pass as an information-exposure rule**, not a
new visual direction. It makes the four quiet pages materially more complete
while largely preserving the baseline's calm first viewport. It does **not**
prove that the quiet lane is more beautiful than the baseline.

The most useful outcome is the separation of two measurements:

| Case | Initial calm / focal clarity — baseline | Initial calm / focal clarity — quiet | Decision / recovery contract — baseline | Decision / recovery contract — quiet | Review result |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| MIRA | 4.4 | 4.4 | 1.7 | 4.2 | Pass: the product, price, and size remain quiet; bag, delivery, exchange, and unavailable-size recovery appear only after a choice. |
| Relay | 4.4 | 4.2 | 1.8 | 4.3 | Conditional: action and owner recovery are materially better, but the quiet lane is slightly sparser than the strong baseline workspace. |
| Rasa | 4.4 | 4.3 | 1.8 | 4.2 | Conditional: urgent escalation remains visible and route selection is real; safety language still needs domain review. |
| ORION | 4.4 | 4.3 | 1.7 | 4.2 | Pass: the vehicle and planning action still lead; time confirmation and unavailable-time choice appear only after request. |
| **Mean** | **4.4** | **4.3** | **1.8** | **4.2** | Quiet lane gains capability without a material calm collapse. |

Scores are qualitative rendered-review scores out of 5. A score of 5 requires
compact-device rendering, real integrations, accessibility testing beyond a
code audit, and human task validation. The scores deliberately do not average
calm and completeness into a single flattering number.

## What changed in the system

The current tuning now requires an **Exposure Map**. Every meaningful fact or
region is classified as always visible, decision-time, action-result,
exception/recovery, or returning context. This change is wired into:

- `craft/product-experience-architecture.md`
- `craft/design-engineering-quality.md`
- `canonical/generation.contract.json`
- `quality-gate/criteria.json`
- `quality-gate/product-experience.review.template.json`
- `skills/prodige-ui-end-to-end/SKILL.md`

The essential constraint is: state and recovery must be implemented and
reachable, but they do not earn permanent first-viewport visibility unless they
change safety, cost, eligibility, orientation, or the current commitment.

## Evidence checked

- Each lane is independently authored; no shared generator or CSS sheet is
  used across the four product pairs.
- Baseline and quiet pages use the same local photo within MIRA, Rasa, and
  ORION pairs. Relay is intentionally a working-state surface without a photo.
- All four quiet pages pass inline JavaScript syntax validation and include
  status messaging, focus-visible styling, active feedback, a native dialog,
  and a reduced-motion path.
- All eight HTML documents have a title, responsive viewport declaration, and
  closing document tag. Local response validation is recorded after publication.
- Desktop screens were visually reviewed in the local browser. Compact layouts
  are implemented and code-inspected, but were not rendered in a device-width
  browser; that remains a known limitation.

## Honest per-case critique

### MIRA

The quiet lane is the clearest success: the opening still looks like a fashion
product page, not an interaction demo. It retains only the garment, price,
selected size, and bag action. The added capacity is observable after action.
The visual gain is not large—which is correct. A denser catalogue-to-detail
journey is still needed before treating this as full commerce proof.

### Relay

The first quiet draft removed so much lower-page context that it read as empty
rather than calm. The rendered audit restored a compact `72% release
confidence` cue because it changes the current rollout decision. It remains a
little sparser than the baseline and should not be promoted as a universal SaaS
layout. The rule is working precisely because it allowed this correction rather
than forcing a no-context minimalism.

### Rasa

Urgent help remains visible at the header and the route can be requested in
plain language. This is quieter than a persistent triage dashboard, but it is
not a clinical product approval: escalation thresholds, medical copy, and
appointment behavior require subject-matter and user testing.

### ORION

The quiet lane removes generic navigation and one unnecessary field while
retaining vehicle proof, location, model, day, and a reversible time request.
Its more technical sans treatment is a product-specific alternative, not a
replacement for the baseline serif by default. Visual preference here is still
subjective; the important pass is that confirmation and unavailability no
longer occupy the idle screen.

## What must not be inferred

- Do not convert “quiet capability” into paper backgrounds, dark ink, serif
  display type, tiny all-caps labels, or less content across every domain.
- Do not hide an urgent, legal, cost, eligibility, consent, or destructive
  consequence merely because it would make the screenshot cleaner.
- Do not declare a page complete because it owns a dialog or status message;
  the state must connect to a truthful product consequence.
- Do not promote this tuning as final until it passes compact rendering and a
  broader transfer set such as dense finance/admin, playful consumer product,
  and public-service content.

## Promotion decision

Promote the **Exposure Map / first-viewport-budget rule** as the next tuning
hypothesis. Do not promote a new visual house style. Preserve a chosen baseline
composition, type behavior, palette, and navigation restraint unless the
product decision supplies a concrete reason to change them.
